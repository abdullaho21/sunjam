# Arabic editorial guide

Use clear contemporary standard Arabic for clues, hints and answers. Keep the existing friendly Gulf voice in navigation and party instructions. Trivia asks for precise recall; it does not need a literary narrator.

- Preserve what the clue asks the player to identify. Reorder the sentence to make the subject, condition and requested answer clear in Arabic.
- Use a familiar Arabic term when it conveys the meaning accurately. If a mechanic, effect or invented proper name becomes unclear in translation, retain a short English term in parentheses, as requested by the user. Example: أفضلية في لفات الهجوم (Advantage), or وقفة البراعة (Virtuose).
- Keep canonical names recognizable. Do not invent Arabic titles that make an item impossible to recognize in the game. English answers and accepted Arabic transliterations may appear together.
- Distinguish a condition from its result: applying twice as many Burn stacks differs from doubling Burn damage. Distinguish a failed attempted procedure from a successful extraction.
- State the game, edition, generation or historical season when the answer depends on it. Ask for all parts explicitly when an answer requires a name plus a condition or number.
- Hints help with recall; they should not silently add a missing condition to the question. An answer should be judgeable without consulting the hint.
- Use 200 for familiar in-world knowledge, 400 for specific events/items/characters, and 600 for optional content, exact mechanics, limitations or less familiar details. Difficulty remains an editorial estimate.

## User-supplied references and access

The primary requested reference is Ahmed Saleh Elimam, *Arabic–English–Arabic Literary Translation: Issues and Strategies* (2023). The legal [publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781474486637_A49301471/preview-9781474486637_A49301471.pdf) was consulted for its introduction and contents; the full textual/contextual chapters were not available. Its attention to style, context and reader expectations informs the approach above.

The [publisher description of Mustafa Mughazy’s Georgetown Guide](https://press.georgetown.edu/Book/The-Georgetown-Guide-to-Arabic-English-Translation) was consulted for its functional approach to translation. The [Routledge Handbook of Arabic Translation](https://www.routledge.com/Routledge-Handbook-of-Arabic-Translation/Hanna-El-Farahaty-Khalifa/p/book/9781138958043) was identified as the user's reference for register and terminology; the full book was not read.

The user also supplied *The Prophet* (bilingual), *Anthology of Arabic Discourse on Translation* (2024), Maan Z. Madina’s dictionary and *The Oxford Arabic Dictionary*. Their full texts were not accessed in this session. No claim is made that the bank has been checked against those dictionaries. Gibran’s literary voice is not imitated in short trivia clues.
